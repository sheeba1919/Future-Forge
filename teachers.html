Purposeful, practical learning

More than a robot.
A way of thinking.

Students in Grades 5–11 apply STEM concepts through authentic design challenges. Guided activities make the day accessible to beginners, while testing and refinement encourage deeper thinking.

DESIGNBUILDCODETESTIMPROVESHARE
Zone 01
LEGO
Line FollowingGRADES 5–11

Build an autonomous LEGO robot that follows a black-line track quickly and accurately, using colour sensors and programmed motor control.

By the end, students should be able to
  • Use a colour sensor to distinguish the line from its surroundings.
  • Apply conditions, loops and motor commands to guide movement.
  • Debug and refine a robot’s code and design to balance speed, accuracy and reliability.
Challenge format & what to look for

Teams may have up to three attempts, depending on time, with rankings based on the fastest valid lap. Ask: “What did you change after testing, and how did it improve your robot?” Look for systematic testing and decisions supported by evidence.

Zone 02
LEGO
SumoGRADES 5–11

Design an autonomous robot that detects and pushes an opponent out of a sumo ring while keeping itself inside the boundary.

By the end, students should be able to
  • Explain how stability, weight distribution and pushing mechanisms affect performance.
  • Use sensors and decision-making logic to detect opponents and avoid the ring boundary.
  • Adapt their strategy after testing, while demonstrating resilience and sportsmanship.
Challenge format & what to look for

Matches consist of up to three rounds of 1.5 minutes each; the first team to win two rounds wins. The tournament includes a losers’ bracket. Ask: “How does your robot decide when to attack or turn?” Look for thoughtful trade-offs between mechanical design and programming.

Zone 03
Robot
EngineeringGRADES 8–11 ONLY

Inspired by FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC). Students combine mechanical construction, guided Java programming and gamepad controls to complete a target-based course.

By the end, students should be able to
  • Assemble a functional motor-driven drivetrain and integrate a movable attachment.
  • Use guided Java code to understand and control motors, movement and the attachment.
  • Drive forward, backward and through turns, then position the mechanism to touch or hit a target.
  • Collaborate across construction and coding roles to test and troubleshoot a working robotic system.
Workshop outcome & what to look for

Students gain experience of building a complete FTC-style robot and seeing how hardware and software work together. Ask: “How does a change in your code affect the mechanism?” Look for effective integration, communication and shared problem-solving.

STEM in action

Sensors, forces, motion, measurement and programming connect classroom ideas to physical results.

Transferable skills

Students practise collaboration, communication, creative thinking, troubleshooting and self-management.

Confidence & future readiness

Students can explain their decisions, reflect on improvement and use their experience and certificate as evidence for their portfolios.
